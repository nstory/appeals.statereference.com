What is a public records appeal?

Under the Massachusetts Public Records Law, anyone can request records from state and local government agencies. Agencies must provide them unless an exemption applies. Exemptions are specific legal reasons for withholding a record.

A requester unhappy with an agency's response may appeal to the Supervisor of Records, an official in the Secretary of the Commonwealth's office. The Supervisor then issues a determination and may order the agency to respond further.

These determinations show how the law is applied in practice.

What is this database?

We download the determinations issued by the Supervisor of Records from 2020 to the present and index them so you can search them online.

Why is this useful?

A few ideas:

1. Are you thinking of making a public records request, but you don't know exactly what to ask for?
Find requests made by previous requesters.

2. Did an agency deny your request?
Search the database for similar requests and see whether the Supervisor upheld those denials.

How is this different from the state's database?

The Secretary of the Commonwealth provides the AppealsWeb interface. It's useful for searching particular fields, and for accessing the older determinations we don't index.

The main advantage of StateReference Appeals is full-text search: we search the entire text of every determination.
